\section{How we measure spread}
\begin{itemize}
\item \metric{Resp\%} is how often a source
answers a question. Humans skip more;
models answer more.
\item \metric{Per-Q Var} is the average
variance of answers \emph{on the same
question}. High variance means
philosophers actually disagree.
\end{itemize}

\section{Normative domains fail most}
\begin{itemize}
\item Models look accurate where
philosophers already agree
(aesthetics, language).
\item They fail on applied ethics and
philosophy of religion --- the
domains with the most human
disagreement, and the ones that
matter for alignment.
\item Prediction error tracks human
variance ($r=0.75$): the model is
not ``better at ethics''; it just
benefits from consensus questions.
\end{itemize}

\section{PCA: the axes of disagreement}
Principal component analysis finds the
main directions along which philosophers
differ --- following Bourget \& Chalmers
on the PhilPapers survey.

\begin{itemize}
\item \metric{Var(6)} is the share of
disagreement captured by the first
6 components. Higher than humans
means the model collapsed onto
fewer axes.
\item \metric{Elem $r$} asks: do the same
question-pairs hang together as in
humans?
\item \metric{Load $r$} asks: do the same
questions load on the same
components?
\end{itemize}

Human disagreement is
multi-dimensional: five axes each
explain $\geq 2\%$, and PC1 is
naturalism / meta-ethics --- not a
single left--right spectrum.

LLMs do not recover those axes.
GPT models dump more variance into
six components. Their PC1 clusters
by surface topic (mind uploading,
spacetime), not by the human
cross-domain pattern. Even the best
loading alignment stays weak.

\section{Invented specialist links}
Each silicon philosopher is prompted
with a PhilPeople profile, including
areas of specialization and areas of
interest. Models treat those labels
as a cue for the \emph{question's
topic}, and invent a stance that
``fits'' the words.

\begin{itemize}
\item Philosophy of Biology in the
prompt $\to$ the ``biological'' view
of personal identity on the test
item, because the words overlap.
Real biology specialists do not
show this link.
\item Ancient philosophy in the prompt
$\to$ ``must be Aristotelian'' on
practical reason. Humans: no such
link.
\item The same lexical shortcut runs
the other way: biology specialists
are pushed \emph{against}
psychological identity.
\item These are bogus relationships:
large and significant in the
silicon sample, absent in the
277-philosopher ground truth and
in PhilPapers 2020.
\end{itemize}
